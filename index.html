1. What is happening and why

Weather versus climate

Weather is one day's conditions. Climate is the average weather of a place over thirty years or more. Climate change is a lasting shift in those averages.

The greenhouse effect

Carbon dioxide and methane trap heat like a blanket. Since the 1800s, burning coal, oil and gas has been the main driver (NASA, IPCC), with deforestation, farming and landfills adding more.

Sunlight reaches the surface; part of the heat is held back by greenhouse gases.

3. How smart devices help: sense, send, act

Every device here repeats one loop. A sensor measures, the data travels to a phone or cloud, and a system acts or advises a person.

Smarter clean energy

Forecasting models predict sun and wind, smart grids balance supply and demand, and grid batteries hold surplus power for peak hours. This targets fossil fuel burning, the largest cause of warming.

SenseWeather and grid load
SendTo grid control software
ActDispatch solar, wind, storage

India now has 300.50 GW of non-fossil capacity, over 54% of installed power (MNRE, July 2026).

Smarter farming

Soil moisture and weather sensors say when to irrigate, drones find crop stress early, and crop residue can become briquettes or biochar instead of smoke.

SenseSoil moisture, rainfall
SendTo a phone app
ActIrrigate only when needed

Punjab farm fires fell from 36,663 in 2023 to 10,909 in 2024 (PPCB).

Satellites and AI for monitoring and early warning

Satellites track ice, forests, heat and methane leaks. AI models turn this into wildfire detection, flood alerts and heat warnings sent to phones.

SenseSatellite and ground data
SendTo analysis models
ActAlert people and agencies

Early warning matters after events like the 2023 Maui wildfire.

Smart homes and buildings

Occupancy sensors switch off empty rooms, smart thermostats learn schedules, and smart meters show water and power leaks.

SensePresence, temperature
SendTo a hub or app
ActCut cooling and lights

ENERGY STAR certified smart thermostats save about 8% of heating and cooling costs on average.

5. The other side: what devices cost

Global e-waste (million tonnes)

Global E-waste Monitor 2024. Only 22.3% of 2022 e-waste was documented as properly collected and recycled.

Data centre electricity (TWh per year)

IEA, Energy and AI (2025). 415 TWh in 2024 is about 1.5% of world electricity. The IEA also notes AI can cut emissions elsewhere.

A device helps only if it saves more than it costs to make, run and dispose of. In India, old electronics should go to authorised collectors under the E-Waste (Management) Rules, 2022.
